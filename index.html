Open quantum systems

I am Chang Shu, a Ph.D. candidate in condensed matter theory working with Prof. Kai Sun at the University of Michigan (UMich).

My research asks what new physics emerges when quantum matter exchanges energy and information with its environment. I study mixed-state order, dynamical phase transitions, non-Hermitian response, and dissipative transport, combining analytical theory with tensor-network and quantum-trajectory simulations.

Research themes

How does openness change order, response, and transport in quantum matter?

01

Mixed-state order and criticality

I study how nonlinear order develops, why symmetry sets its growth, and when quantum jumps preserve or smear dynamical phase transitions under particle loss and gain.

02

Non-Hermitian response

I investigate algebraic boundary localization beyond one dimension and the spectral reconstruction caused by distant, weak impurities. Acoustic experiments connect these predictions to nonlocal response.

03

Dissipative transport

I explore one-way propagation produced by driving and structured loss, and spectral-moment descriptions of bulk dynamics that stay robust as boundary conditions change.

Looking ahead

I aim to engineer local dissipation for rapid, robust preparation of quantum states and protected memory. I also plan to connect mixed-state order with local learnability and error correction, and explore new symmetry-breaking patterns for non-Abelian groups.
