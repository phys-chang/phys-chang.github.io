# Images

All research figures displayed on the website are self-contained SVGs. Their lines, shapes, text, and LaTeX equations are vector paths, with no embedded bitmap labels or external fonts. `profile.png` is the original portrait photograph.

- `lindbladian-tebd.svg` — methodology figure in `../../notes.html`, also previewed on the homepage. It illustrates density-matrix vectorization, an MPS, alternating local channel gates, and SVD truncation, based on Chang Shu's slides. The convention is `\partial_t |\rho\rangle\!\rangle = \mathcal{L}|\rho\rangle\!\rangle`, so `\mathcal{E}(\Delta t) = \exp(\Delta t\,\mathcal{L})`. Method reference: [Zwolak & Vidal (2004)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0406440).
- `quantum-trajectories.svg` — Monte Carlo wavefunction figure in `../../notes.html`. Its source is [Section IV of the DQPT supplement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03570v2#page=13), Eqs. Panel 02 aligns each probability directly over its outcome. In panel 03, smooth blue curves show no-jump evolution and prominent warm red vertical arrows show abrupt jumps, following Chang Shu's sketch. The histories are schematic paths through state space; vertical position is not the wavefunction norm or a computed scalar observable.

- `swssb.svg` — the first Research Story, based on [arXiv:2603.06363v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06363v2). Its larger 1672 × 4400 canvas gives most of the space to explaining SWSSB. Panel 00 introduces strong versus weak finite-state symmetry before Panel 01 explicitly labels the ordinary/nonlinear comparison as **Global SWSSB**, with a prominent reference band for [Lessa et al., PRX Quantum 6, 010344 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.6.010344) ([arXiv:2405.03639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03639)). The charge-learnability block is labelled **Local SWSSB**, with a prominent reference band for Divi, Lessa and Wang ([arXiv:2605.28967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28967)), additional local diagnostic references to Carolyn Zhang (2605.29113) and Tang, Kattel and Pixley (2606.02713), a charge-scrambling reference to Jong Yeon Lee (2605.05288), and a related reference to Kumar et al. (2602.11262). A nested-set diagram distinguishes local/global fidelity SWSSB. Panel 02 is the spreading-order waterfall. The original diagnostics and waterfall use the `H=0`, `L_j=\sqrt{\gamma}Z_jZ_{j+1}` example of Appendix A, starting from the all-`+x` state. The late-time state reached from this initial condition is `\rho_+=(I+X)/2^L`, with `X=\prod_jX_j`. Global parity stays fixed in this evolution.
- `ultrasensitivity.svg` — a 1672 × 2420 Research Story based on [Chang Shu, Kai Zhang and Kai Sun, *Ultra spectral sensitivity and non-local bi-impurity bound states from quasi-long-range non-hermitian skin modes*, arXiv:2409.13623v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13623v1). It compares one and two weak impurities in a reciprocal 2D non-Hermitian cylinder, explains the off-resonance forth-back Green-function response, and illustrates nonlocal bound states and wavepacket revivals. The complex-spectrum points are a numerical reproduction of the model; the lattice/cylinder, propagation, and dynamics drawings are schematic. Panel 01 uses shaded blue surfaces, a beveled near opening, and soft ground shadows for depth; the shading consists entirely of vector color bands. The surface, mesh, and rim share one orthographic projection: the near left opening is visible, while the opaque sidewall hides the far opening, leaving only its front boundary arc. The aligned defect positions and open-axial/periodic-circumferential geometry are preserved.
- `ultrasensitivity-preview.svg` — a standalone 1672 × 941 vector viewport of panel 01 from `ultrasensitivity.svg`, used as a full-width homepage Research Story preview. Its intrinsic aspect ratio matches the card, preserving the large illustration without translating the tall figure in CSS.

Panel 00 uses the same `Z_2` parity `Q=prod_j X_j` as the main example. Each framed row is an orthogonal X-basis component of an illustrative classical mixture, not a measurement snapshot or a row in a 2D lattice. The strong example has configurations `++++`, `--++`, and `+-+-`, all with `q=+1`. The weak-only mixture includes `++++`, `-+++`, and `+-++`, spanning both charge sectors with no coherence between sectors. Its symmetry is conjugation invariance `Q rho_w Q^dagger=rho_w`; the strong example obeys `Q rho_s=q rho_s` and therefore also weak symmetry. These are finite-state symmetry definitions, not phases separated by charge-mixing dynamics. The strongly symmetric fixed-charge states discussed below can develop SWSSB through thermodynamic-limit nonlinear order. Source: [Lessa et al., Introduction Eqs. (1)–(2) and Definition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.03639v3), and [the research paper, Section II](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06363v2#S2).

In panel 01, three staggered cards labelled **Shot 1/2/3** each show one independent **Z-measurement outcome** from a fresh preparation of the same 1D state. Bonds run only within each card's chain; the separate frames and offset positions distinguish copies from a 2D lattice. The blue arrows are schematic measurement outcomes, not an ensemble decomposition: the state retains global off-diagonal Z-basis coherence. The redesigned nonlinear view follows [Sala, Gopalakrishnan, Oshikawa and You, *Spontaneous strong symmetry breaking in open systems: Purification perspective*, PRB 110, 155150 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.110.155150), [arXiv:2405.02402v1, Section III.1 / Figure 3 / Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02402v1#S3.SS1). Blue system sites and gold bond ancillas occupy two separately framed purifications, `|Psi>` and its conjugate `|Psi*>`. Green bridges denote coherent Bell postselection on every environment pair, yielding the normalized remaining system state `|rho>>/sqrt(Tr rho^2)`. This is a postselected vectorized state. Its paired system operators `O_i=Z_i^(1)Z_i^(2)` have correlation `R_2(i,j)=<O_i O_j>_post`; their green link represents operator correlation. The illustrated exact pair-dephasing steady-state limits remain `C_Z(r>0)=0` and `R_2(r>0)=1`. The Sala citation appears directly in the global figure band and as a linked citation in the caption. In the flip-test inset, `Z_i` flips an **X-basis** spin: `Z_i\rho_+Z_i=\rho_-=(I-X)/2^L`, which is orthogonal to `\rho_+`, while `Z_iZ_j\rho_+Z_jZ_i=\rho_+` even for distant sites. The latter permutes individual configurations within the original ensemble. The one-flip test is included to distinguish nonlinear order from simple pair invariance of a completely mixed state.

The local-learnability view follows [Divi, Lessa and Wang, arXiv:2605.28967v1, Introduction and Section III](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28967v1). Both columns compare `\rho` with the same charge-changing probe `\sigma=Z_i\rho Z_i`, with the insertion **inside** the local patch `A`. Left: the all-`+x` product state and its single-flip partner have opposite global parities and orthogonal local states, so measuring `X_i` identifies the charge change (`F_A=0`). The plus/minus symbols label X eigenstates. Right: the fully scrambled fixed-point sectors `\rho_\pm=(I\pm Q)/2^L`, with `Q=\prod_jX_j`, have `\rho_A=\sigma_A=I_A/2^{|A|}` on every proper subset (`F_A=1`). Neutral site circles represent identical local mixed states, not individual outcomes. The gold arrows denote the same local probe, not charge-preserving evolution.

Here `F_A=F(\rho_A,\sigma_A)` is root fidelity. In the local infinite-system formulation, the growing-window limit vanishes in an unbroken strong phase and is nonzero for local SWSSB. Global fidelity SWSSB implies the local criterion; the converse need not hold. Generic SWSSB only prevents perfect local discrimination, rather than requiring exactly identical patch states. Do not assert that any arbitrary patch reveals the absolute charge of an arbitrary strongly symmetric state: a flip outside the patch is invisible even in a product state. [Unlearnable phases of matter, arXiv:2602.11262v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11262v2) provides a complementary result for learning global charge from local statistical queries; monitored charge learning instead uses a time-dependent measurement record. These are related but distinct protocols.

The nested-set diagram uses the **fidelity definitions**, separately from the Rényi-2 profiles: [Theorem II.1 and Section II.3 of arXiv:2605.28967v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28967v1#S2) show that global SWSSB implies local SWSSB, with counterexamples to the converse. The outer blue set is local SWSSB; the inner green set is global SWSSB. Locally thermal, high-energy pure ETH eigenstates with no ordinary symmetry breaking occupy the local-only region. The comparison takes the thermodynamic limit before the growing-window limit (`1 << ell_A << L`). Set areas have no quantitative meaning. This containment should not be interpreted as a theorem about arbitrary Rényi-2 proxies.

Additional local diagnostic references are [Carolyn Zhang, *Local diagnostics for strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking and non-equilibrium phase transitions*, arXiv:2605.29113v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29113), which introduces marginal fidelity correlators evaluated on local regions, and [Yicheng Tang, Pradip Kattel and J. H. Pixley, *A mean-field description of strong-to-weak symmetry breaking in the monitored Bose-Hubbard model*, arXiv:2606.02713v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02713v2), which uses a trajectory-averaged local order parameter within a Gutzwiller mean-field description. The latter citation follows the revised title of September 12, 2026. These complement the local one-point fidelity formulation of Divi, Lessa and Wang; neither is cited as the source of the universal local/global containment theorem. The model-specific mean-field trajectory diagnostic should not be conflated with that local fidelity definition.

[Jong Yeon Lee (UIUC), *Charge Scrambling in Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking*, arXiv:2605.05288v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05288) adds a complementary view through subsystem charge fluctuations. For continuous symmetry, sufficiently fast convergence of long-range Rényi-1 order implies an extensive charge-variance lower bound; the implication is conditional and the converse fails. This reference does not replace the local one-point fidelity definition or its global-to-local implication. The separate website reference block is removed; citations appear in the figure bands, with regular-weight citation headings.

The SWSSB waterfall contains analytic profiles `R_2(r,t)=[\tanh(2\gamma t)]^{|r|}` at `\gamma t=0.30,0.45,\ldots,1.20`, with signed displacement `r=i-j` and range `\xi=-1/\ln[\tanh(2\gamma t)]`. Ordinary separated-site `Z` correlations vanish in this introductory example. These profiles are not TEBD simulation data. The small final comparison summarizes the exponential `\mathbb{Z}_2` and algebraic `U(1)` growth established in Sections III–IV. Do not apply the zero ordinary-correlation claim to every model: the original gapless `\mathbb{Z}_2` example has an intermediate SWSSB window before its eventual GHZ state also develops conventional order. Onset times are effective finite-size scales; the infinite-chain transition is asymptotic.

The ultrasensitivity story follows Sections II–V and [Methods I–II](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13623v1#S6). For the clean Hamiltonian `H_0`, define `G_ij(E)=<r_i|(E-H_0)^(-1)|r_j>` at an energy outside its spectrum and `P_forth-back=G_12 G_21`. For equal onsite defect strengths `V`, the nonlocal response scale is `|V| sqrt(|P_forth-back|)`. The exact two-defect secular condition is `(1-V G_11)(1-V G_22)-V^2 G_12 G_21=0`; the inverse square root of the round-trip magnitude gives the perturbative threshold scale when the local response stays bounded. A response growing in one direction alone does not guarantee this effect: both Green-function factors matter.

Its spectrum plots are independently computed for a **40 × 40** cylinder with `t_x=i`, `t_y=0`, `t_xy=3`, `u=-2i`, and onsite potential `V=0.01` at `(0,0)` or both `(0,0)` and `(39,0)` (zero-based coordinates). The source paper's Fig. 1 uses 60 × 60, so these are a finite-size reproduction rather than its original data. Gold points highlight the 20 two-defect eigenvalues at least 0.15 from the nearest clean eigenvalue; this threshold only selects points for visual emphasis. The maximum nearest-clean-spectrum distance is about `2.68e-5` for one defect and `0.783` for two. The clean spectrum was checked against the exact Fourier-channel formula; the separated induced states satisfy the independent two-site Green-function secular equation to residual below `2.6e-12`. Raw spectra, generation macros, and validation metadata are in `../figures/ultrasensitivity-spectrum-*`. To regenerate these numerical data, run `python3 scripts/ultrasensitivity_spectrum.py` from the repository root with NumPy, SciPy and threadpoolctl installed; ordinary SVG rebuilding reuses the checked-in coordinates.

The example is reciprocal (`H_0^T=H_0`) with open boundaries in `x` and periodic boundaries in `y`. Aspect ratio `alpha=L_x/L_y` and defect offset `g=Delta y/L_x` control whether the round trip diverges as separation grows. Arbitrarily weak defects can become nonperturbative only in an appropriate sensitive thermodynamic limit; this is not a universal claim for every non-Hermitian model or every placement. The clean cylinder's fixed-transverse-momentum channels have exponentially localized skin modes. The **induced bi-impurity states** have algebraic tails, as discussed in Supplementary Note IV; do not label all clean cylinder eigenstates as algebraic. The wavepacket-revival panel depicts repeated bulk scattering with slower decay, not growth of the norm or a numerical time-evolution trace.

## Editing and rebuilding

Edit the LaTeX/TikZ sources in `../figures/`. Colors, typography, line widths, panel headings, and process strips are shared in `../figures/figure-style.tex`. From the repository root, run:

```sh
python3 scripts/build_figures.py
```

The builder requires Python 3, LaTeX with `standalone`, `newtx`, and TikZ, and `dvisvgm` on `PATH`. It outlines every glyph and checks that exports contain no raster content or external dependencies. To rebuild one figure, pass its basename, for example `python3 scripts/build_figures.py quantum-trajectories`.

The earlier Imagegen PNGs and `.prompt.txt` files remain as design references. `open-system-placeholder.svg` and `open-system.svg` are archived; research figures displayed on the website use the vector exports.

## Shared figure style

Use `lindbladian-tebd.svg` and the shared TikZ styles as the reference for new figures.

- Match the margins, pale ice-blue background, and navy serif typography. Methodology figures use a 1672 × 941 landscape canvas; simple schematics can use a shorter canvas, and research stories can use a taller canvas to explain unfamiliar concepts.
- Use blue outlines and pale-blue fills for states, sage-green operators, and small gold process arrows. Quantum-trajectory jump arrows use a warm red accent to distinguish abrupt jumps from smooth blue evolution. Keep line widths, corner radii, and subtle shadows consistent.
- Keep numbered panel badges, pale-blue separators, label sizes, and the bottom process strip consistent where the scientific narrative uses them.
- Use a modest descriptive subtitle; put the method or paper title in the webpage heading.
- Write every equation in LaTeX and export glyphs as paths. Label schematic histories as such and use actual data when displaying numerical results.
